Recent graduates often struggle with finding jobs or internships that match their skills, education, and experience level. My proposal is to develop a recommendation/matching system specifically for recent graduates in the tech field. The system will take a résumé as input and return a ranked list of appropriate tech jobs and internships. Each recommendation will include a brief explanation and a score from 0–100 indicating how strongly the résumé and job posting match.

The main research question is whether fine-tuning an LLM for résumé–job matching improves its matching accuracy and explanation quality. I will fine-tune a small instruction model such as Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with LoRA, focusing on recent graduate résumés and entry-level tech positions. The goal is to help the model better use technical qualifications, such as programming languages, tools, and related skills, when assessing a match. This is fine-tuning for a specific task within the tech field.

I will evaluate the fine-tuned model against the base model and a general-purpose model accessed through an API. Each model will match the same résumés against the same sets of job postings. The model’s task will be to recommend suitable postings, explain the matches, and provide matching scores. Keyword overlap will be one evaluation measure, rather than the task itself.

For keyword evaluation, I will manually identify relevant skills in the résumés and job descriptions, accounting for capitalization and common aliases. I will calculate the percentage of a posting’s required skills that appear in the résumé. For example, if an internship requires Python, SQL, Excel, and Tableau, and the résumé lists Python, SQL, and Excel, the keyword score will be 3/4, or 75%. I will compare these scores across the models’ top recommendations to examine whether fine-tuning produces recommendations with greater skill overlap. This measure gives equal weight to each skill and does not account for proficiency, experience, or transferable skills, so it will be used alongside other measures.

I will obtain job postings from the LinkedIn Job Postings dataset, which contains descriptions and associated posting information from 2023–2024. I will clean and filter the dataset to select entry-level tech positions and internships, checking experience and eligibility requirements. These postings will support development and evaluation. Using current postings through available exports or feeds from LinkedIn and other job boards will be an extension after the initial system is working.

For initial fine-tuning, I will use the Resume–Job Fit dataset from Hugging Face, which contains 2,385 résumé–job instances with skill-matching information and scores. Its AI-generated scores will serve as approximate training labels. Because the dataset covers only 23 distinct roles and includes experienced applicants, I will identify the subset relevant to tech and recent graduates before training. I will separate training and evaluation résumés and remove duplicates to reduce data leakage.

I expect fine-tuning to improve matching accuracy over the untuned model. In addition to keyword overlap, I will manually assess whether recommendations meet education, experience, and eligibility requirements. I will evaluate whether explanations accurately describe matching qualifications and missing skills, and whether the model’s scores agree with a consistent manual assessment rubric. The model’s score will represent qualification alignment, rather than hiring probability. I will also record response time and API costs, using basic Colab resources and an API budget of approximately $20.

For the midterm report, I will download the training and testing datasets, implement the LLM, and demonstrate that the LoRA training implementation can run. Performance results will not be required at that stage.
